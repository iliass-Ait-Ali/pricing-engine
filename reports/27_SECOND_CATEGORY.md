# 27 - Second category: do the Cereals findings hold in Crackers?

Generated by `python scripts/compare_categories.py --config crackers.yaml` on
2026-10-05. Both runs use the same code and
**identical** decision settings (policy profiles, risk bands, model, eligibility and elasticity settings).
Only the input files differ.

> **What this is.** A robustness check: the unchanged pipeline on a second
> product category from the same retailer and years. It shows whether the
> headline findings are specific to Cereals. It is not proof that they hold for
> other retailers, decades or markets, and every value figure is still a
> model-internal estimate.

## Answer first

**7 of 8 headline findings point the same way in Crackers.** Findings that do not hold in both: the model-internal value range is positive at its low end.

The central one: in Cereals, 2.9% of final prices come
from the model's own optimum; in Crackers it is 1.4%.
A rule that ignores demand lands within one 5-cent step of the engine in
82.5% of Cereals contexts and 91.6%
of Crackers contexts. The description "rule-bounded pricing with a learned
direction" therefore fits both.

**The one that does not hold matters.** In Cereals the recommendations keep a
positive model-internal value under every evidenced elasticity
(+4.1% at worst). In Crackers the low end is -0.1%: if
shoppers are as price-sensitive as the steepest evidence says, the same price
increases earn nothing. The engine's own estimate there (+3.3%) is
also less than half the Cereals figure. So "the recommendations are worth
something even in the worst case" is a Cereals result, not a general one, which
is one more reason to measure with a pilot before any rollout.

## Reading the differences

* Numbers differ between categories, as they should: different products,
  margins and promotion habits. What matters is the direction of each finding.
* The constraint that binds first differs: `MAX_PRICE_CHANGE` in Cereals,
  `EXTRAPOLATION` in Crackers. Which rule does the work depends on how
  much each category's prices have moved in the past.
* The crackers file contains 200 blank export rows (a UPC and nothing else),
  which the cereal file does not. The loader now reads them, and the cleaning
  step counts and drops them under their own rule (`blank_key_rows`); the
  cereal table is byte-identical to before that change.
* Row-level outputs of the second run stay in git-ignored folders
  (`data/processed/`, `artifacts_crackers/`). Only this comparison is
  committed.
